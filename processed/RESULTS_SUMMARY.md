`scripts/03_plots.py`. This is the auditable numeric summary for Chapter 4; every figure
below is traceable to a CSV/JSON file in `processed/`.

## Step 7: Sleep-phase energy (analytical, datasheet-derived)

```
E_sleep = V_sleep x I_sleep x T_sleep = 3.3 V x 5 uA x 60 s = 990 uJ
```

**Datasheet citation (as cited in the methodology):** Espressif *ESP32-C3 Series
Datasheet, Version 2.3*, Table 5-9 "Current Consumption in Low-Power Modes" — Deep-sleep
(RTC timer + RTC memory) = **5 µA (Typ)**. Per §5.6.1, current-consumption measurements in
that section are taken with a **3.3 V supply at 25 °C ambient**, matching the `V_SLEEP =
3.3 V` used here.

Sleep-timer deviation (1 s used during collection vs 60 s design value) does not affect any
reported metric, per the justification in the design docs — do not mention it in the
thesis text.

## Step 9: Comparative statistics

### Per-size significance (Welch's one-tailed t-test, α = 0.01, 100 sizes each)

| Metric | Sizes significant | Proportion |
|---|---|---|
| `time_us` | 21 / 100 | 21% |
| `power_mW` | 78 / 100 | 78% |
| `energy_per_byte_uJ` | 72 / 100 | 72% |

Interpretation: execution time is close between the two algorithms at most payload sizes
(only 21% significantly different), but power draw and per-byte energy differ
significantly at the large majority of sizes — the RQ's energy story is carried by power,
not raw speed.

### Break-even / crossover payload size (Objective 3)

**Primary crossover: ≈ 347 bytes** (interpolated between 336 B and 352 B), where
ASCON's per-byte energy stops being lower than AES's and AES becomes more efficient from
that point on. This holds identically whether using active-phase energy or total
duty-cycle energy (adding the constant sleep term does not shift the crossover, as
expected).

A second near-zero wobble appears around 528–560 B (`ascon_minus_aes_energy` crosses zero
twice within a ±0.003 µJ/byte band) — this is **noise from the two curves converging to
near-parity in that region**, not a genuine second regime change. Report only the ≈347 B
crossover as the headline figure; mention the 528–560 B convergence qualitatively if
discussing curve shape.

### Scaling / regression (linear runtime scaling claim, §2.2.5)

| Algorithm | time slope (µs/byte) | time R² | energy slope (µJ/byte) | energy intercept (µJ) | energy R² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ASCON-128 | 1.197 | 0.99999997 | 0.1068 | 15.03 | 0.9105 |
| AES-128-GCM | 0.901 | 0.99999998 | 0.0335 | 30.14 | 0.4275 |

Both algorithms show essentially perfect linear scaling of execution time with payload
size (R² ≈ 1), directly supporting the sponge-duplexing linear-scaling literature claim.
Energy vs. size is linear but noisier (R² 0.91 / 0.43) since power draws more variance
than time. **The AES energy regression intercept (30.14 µJ) is markedly higher than
ASCON's (15.03 µJ) — this is the hardware AES engine's near-constant per-operation
init/setup overhead (Objective 1), independent of payload size.**

### Memory footprint (Objective 1 context)

- ASCON-128 mean `mem_total`: **2,772.8 bytes**
- AES-128-GCM mean `mem_total`: **3,441.0 bytes**
- AES-GCM (mbedTLS context) uses **~668 bytes (24%) more** memory than ASCON on average,
  fairly constant across payload sizes — supports treating this as a discrete bar
  comparison rather than a scaling relationship.

### Regime segmentation (Objective 3)

| Regime | Mean (ASCON − AES) energy/byte | More efficient |
|---|---|---|
| Small (<256 B, LPWAN-typical) | −0.225 µJ/byte | **ASCON** |
| Large (>800 B, bulk-telemetry-typical) | +0.065 µJ/byte | **AES** |
